The Missile Man of India
Biography

From Rameswaram to Rashtrapati Bhavan

A humble beginning, a scientific career of national importance, and a presidency remembered for its closeness to the people.

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. After studying aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology, he devoted his career to India's space and defence programmes.

His work carried him from laboratories and launch sites to the highest constitutional office in India. Yet he continued to identify first as a teacher—meeting students, asking them to think boldly, and placing national development in the hands of young citizens.

1931–1958

Roots and education

Born into a modest family in Rameswaram, he studied physics and later specialised in aeronautical engineering.

1958–1982

A space programme takes flight

After beginning at DRDO, he joined ISRO and became Project Director of SLV-III, which placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit in 1980.

1982–1999

Building strategic capability

He led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme and later served as Scientific Adviser to the Defence Minister and Secretary of Defence R&D.

2002–2007

The People's President

As India's 11th President, he brought science, education, accessibility, and a citizen-centred vision into public life.

2007–2015

A teacher until the end

He returned to teaching and public engagement, continuing to inspire students until his final lecture at IIM Shillong on 27 July 2015.

Service to India

Science in the service of self-reliance

His legacy connects technology with national confidence, development, and human possibility.

01

SLV-III & Rohini

Led the project that developed India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle, successfully placing Rohini in orbit in 1980.

02

Guided missiles

Guided the integrated programme behind strategic systems including Agni and Prithvi, strengthening indigenous capability.

03

National security

Played a central coordinating role in strategic programmes, including the Pokhran-II tests, while advancing defence self-reliance.

04

Technology Vision 2020

Helped shape a national roadmap that placed technology, education, agriculture, infrastructure, and health at the heart of development.

Ideas for a developed India

His vision was bigger than technology

Kalam imagined development as a shared mission—uniting knowledge, opportunity, character, and inclusive progress.

Empowered youth

Young people should be encouraged to question, create, lead, and turn ambitious goals into useful action.

Education with purpose

Learning should build curiosity, skill, courage, and a sense of responsibility—not simply produce credentials.

Sustainable development

Economic progress must improve rural life, preserve natural resources, and reach citizens beyond major cities.

Scientific temper

Innovation and evidence-based thinking can help solve national challenges and build technological independence.

Technology for society

Science matters most when it improves healthcare, access, mobility, communication, and everyday dignity.

National unity

Shared purpose can rise above differences when citizens place service, peace, and the nation's progress first.

Ethics & character

Principles he lived by

His public life was marked by simplicity, disciplined effort, respect for every person, and an unwavering belief in the power of a worthy goal.

01

Dream with direction

A dream becomes valuable when it leads to thought, planning, and persistent work.

02

Own failure, share success

Leadership means protecting the team during setbacks and giving them credit when the mission succeeds.

03

Live simply, think greatly

Personal modesty can coexist with immense ambition for society and the nation.

04

Knowledge must serve

Expertise earns its meaning when it improves lives and opens opportunity for others.
